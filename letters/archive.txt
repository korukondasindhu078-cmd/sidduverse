THE ARCHIVE

2029 — THE LAST LETTER

Hey, Sidduuuu.

So, here we are. 2029.

If you've opened this letter, you've officially reached the last one. Three years, three letters, and apparently I really did commit to this little idea. Quite impressive, if I say so myself.

I wonder how much has changed since the first letter.

Where are you now? What does your life look like? Are you doing the things you once talked about? Have you travelled to those places you wanted to visit? Have you become the person you imagined you'd be?

And most importantly, have you finally learnt to make a decision without taking forever? I'm beginning to think that's a permanent feature.

I wish I could know all the little details about your life right now. The good days, the difficult ones, the unexpected achievements, the random things that made you laugh. I hope there have been plenty of reasons to smile.

It's been a while since those college days, hasn't it?

The places that once felt familiar might look different now. The people we saw almost every day may have become occasional names in our conversations. The routines we never thought much about might now be things we remember with a smile.

Life really does move on.

And somewhere along the way, we move with it.

I don't know what place I hold in your life now, Sidduuuu. Maybe we're still part of each other's stories. Maybe we're just two people who once shared a very special chapter. Either way, I hope there is no bitterness when you think of those days.

Because I don't want to remember everything through the way it ended, or through the things that didn't work out.

I'd rather remember the laughter, the little conversations, the familiar faces, the photographs and all those moments that made that chapter ours.

I hope I remember them kindly, and I hope you do too.

You were someone I cared about deeply. Someone whose little habits, random expressions and ordinary presence became familiar to me in ways I probably never explained properly.

And even if life has changed everything around us, I won't pretend that those feelings and memories meant nothing.

You were, and will always remain, one of the dearest people I've known.

I don't say that to hold on to the past. I say it because it's true.

And one last time, at least through these letters…

HAPPY BIRTHDAY, SIDDUUUU! ❤️

I hope life continues to surprise you in the loveliest ways. May you find the courage to chase what you want, the patience to get through difficult days and enough little joys to make the ordinary ones special.

I hope you look back at this year and feel proud of how far you've come.

You deserve good things, Sidduuuu. I really hope you get to experience them.

I hope you've grown into someone you're proud of. I hope you're healthy, happy, surrounded by good people and doing something that makes you feel alive.

And if life hasn't turned out exactly as you planned, that's okay too. You don't have to have everything figured out by a certain age. Just keep going, keep learning and don't forget to enjoy the life you're building.

Now, before this gets any more emotional, let me remind you that you're still responsible for at least some of my eye-rolling moments. I hope you've improved. But knowing you, I wouldn't bet on it.

Anyway, Sidduuuu, this is where my little three-year project ends.

No more letters waiting in the future. No more scheduled messages from an earlier version of me.

Just this one, sitting here as a small collection of things I wanted you to know.

Thank you for being a part of my story.

For the memories, the laughter, the lessons, and even the moments that taught me things about myself.

I hope life gives you beautiful experiences, wonderful people and countless reasons to be happy.

And whenever you happen to remember this little universe, I hope you smile.

Not because you have to remember everything, but because some things are simply nice to look back on.

Take care of yourself, Sidduuuu.

Keep becoming, keep exploring and keep finding your own little reasons to love life.

This is the last letter, but it doesn't make the memories any less real.

With all the affection I wanted these pages to carry.
